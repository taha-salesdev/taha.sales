How to Find SaaS Decision Makers for Cold Outreach

By Taha Bin Ishfaq  |  August 30, 2026  |  6 min read

SaaS companies are one of the most active B2B buyer segments — but they're also among the most outreach-saturated. To cut through, you need to reach the right decision maker, not just any LinkedIn connection at the company. Here's how to find them.

Know Which Title Buys in SaaS

Decision-maker titles vary by what you're selling to a SaaS company:

  • Sales tools / CRM: VP of Sales, Head of Revenue, Chief Revenue Officer
  • Marketing tools: VP of Marketing, Head of Growth, CMO
  • HR/People tools: VP of People, Head of Talent, CHRO
  • Dev/Infrastructure tools: CTO, Head of Engineering, VP of Product
  • Lead generation / SDR services: VP of Sales, Head of Business Development, Founder (at smaller companies)

Use Apollo for SaaS-Specific Filtering

Apollo.io lets you filter by "Software" industry with sub-categories. Combine with company headcount (10–200 for growth-stage SaaS), funding status, and job title to get a precise list. You can also filter by technology stack — useful for identifying companies that already use tools in your category.

LinkedIn Sales Navigator for Precision

In Sales Navigator, filter by "Software and Internet" industry, company size, and a specific list of target titles. Use the "Posted on LinkedIn in last 30 days" filter to identify people who are active on the platform — they're far more likely to respond to InMail or connection requests.

G2 and Capterra for Intent Signals

SaaS buyers actively research solutions on G2 and Capterra before purchasing. Both platforms offer buyer intent data — companies actively browsing your category. This is powerful signal for timing your outreach to when they're already in evaluation mode.

What Makes SaaS Outreach Convert

SaaS buyers are sophisticated and see hundreds of cold emails. What cuts through:

  • Reference specific pain points common in their stage of growth
  • Mention relevant results from similar SaaS companies you've worked with
  • Keep it short — SaaS buyers don't read long emails
  • Personalize the first line based on their recent activity or company news
