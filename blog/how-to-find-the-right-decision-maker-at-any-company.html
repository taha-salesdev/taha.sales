How to Find the Right Decision Maker at Any Company

By Taha Bin Ishfaq  |  August 30, 2026  |  6 min read

One of the biggest mistakes in B2B outreach is contacting the wrong person. You can have the perfect email, the perfect offer, and the perfect timing — and still get ignored because you reached out to someone who has no authority to say yes.

Finding the right decision maker isn't about luck. It's a repeatable process once you know what to look for. Here's how I approach it for every client project.

1. Start With the Buying Role, Not the Job Title

Different products get bought by different people. Before searching for a contact, ask: who signs off on this purchase? Common decision-maker types in B2B include:

  • Economic buyer: Controls the budget — often a VP, Director, or C-suite.
  • Technical buyer: Evaluates if it works — IT manager, Head of Ops, etc.
  • Champion: The person who wants what you're selling and can influence the economic buyer.

Know your buying role first, then search by title.

2. Use LinkedIn to Map the Org Chart

LinkedIn is the most reliable way to understand who holds what responsibility at a target company. Search by company + title to find:

  • Who runs Sales, Marketing, or Operations
  • Which titles report to which titles (gives you the hierarchy)
  • How long the person has been in their role (tenure matters for receptiveness)

3. Cross-Reference With Apollo or ZoomInfo

Once you've identified the right title on LinkedIn, use Apollo.io or ZoomInfo to pull their verified email and direct phone number. These tools let you filter by company, title, seniority, and department — making it easy to zero in on the right person at scale.

4. Check the Company Website

Many mid-size companies list their leadership team or department heads on their About or Team page. This is often overlooked but gives you verified names and sometimes even direct emails without needing any tool.

5. Look for Signal-Based Triggers

The best time to reach a decision maker is when they're actively aware of the problem you solve. Watch for signals like:

  • Recent job change (new leader = new budget)
  • Company growth or funding announcements
  • Job postings that hint at pain points (e.g., hiring an SDR = need more leads)
